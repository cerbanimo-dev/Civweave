# Architecture and constitutional mapping

## Product hypothesis

One candidate community can complete one bounded charter lifecycle without depending on public Internet, remote authentication, a central host, or a sole operator:

1. document the observed bottleneck and a non-software comparator;
2. draft or import charter text;
3. inspect and version it;
4. document proposals and declared outcomes;
5. preserve dissent;
6. map duties, powers, data, threats, and alternate paths;
7. export selected classes;
8. restore or fork on replaceable infrastructure;
9. retain conflicting records for human reconciliation;
10. stop when readiness or rights gates fail.

## Layers

### L0: manual practice

The UI generates language and records that can be copied to paper or plain files. Offline receipts use random act references rather than identity. Every digital workflow is paired with a field for an assisted or non-digital path.

### L1: documented file exchange

`anarchadia.bundle.v1` is a manifest plus selected payload classes. It includes omissions, audience, privacy marking, onward-export rule, conflict status, replica uncertainty, disclaimer, and a SHA-256 payload checksum.

The checksum detects accidental or deliberate file change. It does not establish legitimacy, authority, identity, consent, or priority.

### Deferred higher levels

No relay, sync, shared identity, federation, automatic conflict resolution, server authority, or mandatory sibling bridge exists in the MVP.

## Power model

The software has four categories of behavior:

- **Record:** store community-provided text and declared state.
- **Compare:** display alternatives, versions, diffs, or conflicts without ranking them.
- **Warn:** identify missing fields, prohibited bridge content, or possible rights-critical terms.
- **Refuse:** block live bridge activation and label incomplete rights actions as not executable.

It does not execute decisions, allocate material resources, determine standing, identify members, or infer legitimacy.

## AI boundary

AI is a drafting and inspection subsystem with no direct state mutation path.

Flow:

1. participant selects a task and context;
2. adapter produces an advisory draft;
3. draft appears outside the authoritative record;
4. participant may copy it or explicitly preserve it in AI draft history;
5. a separate human edit is required to change charter, proposal, right, role, data, or threat records.

The system prompt prohibits political profiling, motive inference, legitimacy claims, automatic ratification, and weakening of dissent, exit, privacy, offline, review, remedy, expiry, or replacement protections.

## Conflict behavior

Imports are additive where IDs are new. When the same ID has divergent content, the current and incoming versions are stored in a contested conflict object. Neither is overwritten and the interface provides no “latest wins” button.

A future human reconciliation process may create a new record that cites both predecessors. The MVP deliberately does not automate that process.

## Data boundaries

No analytics, telemetry, crash upload, remote fonts, CDN scripts, accounts, contact access, camera access, location access, or stable cross-community identifier is used.

Community references and record IDs are random, locally generated, purpose-scoped references. They are not identity claims.

## PWA continuity

The service worker caches only same-origin application assets. Runtime AI requests are never cached by the service worker because cross-origin requests are ignored. Manual export remains necessary because Cache Storage and IndexedDB can be cleared by the browser or operating system.

## Source article mapping

- Article 01: bounded journey, disclaimers, admission card, local-first, no-build gate.
- Article 02: refusal, withdrawal, assistance, alternate paths, minimized receipts.
- Article 03: no identity or recovery subsystem.
- Article 04: rights duty cards and `not executable` state.
- Article 05: data and authority map, selective export manifest.
- Article 06: documentation-only procedure cards and no live tallies.
- Article 07: visible role cards, scope, prohibited acts, expiry, recall, replacement.
- Article 12: L0/L1 architecture, no remote dependency, checksum as integrity only.
- Article 13: threat register, explicit unresolved dispositions, deployment blockers.
- Article 14: default-off bridge contracts and minimized Cerbanimo handoff.
- Article 15: manual fork and source provenance; no federation runtime.
- Article 17: synthetic fixture, no telemetry, bounded packet.
- Article 18: version snapshots, amendment-ready exact comparison, expiry and rollback fields.
