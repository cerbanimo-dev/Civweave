# The First Peace — Post-Warlock Coda (Author Review Scene)
**The Blade & Mirror Saga · Warlock aftermath / Convergence transition**
**Author:** Cami Ryn Tharp · **Status:** manuscript insert, not merged into the current canonical editable master
**Narration:** Maggie/Magnus with Landry/Delia responses. Keep the saga's reserved final expletive out of this scene.

After the Warlock's withdrawal, the Commons had acquired the peculiar quality of a room everyone was waiting to evacuate.

Magnus was trying to repair a communications panel that didn't need repairing when Cami's message arrived.

*Please call everyone up for a meeting and craft a door for me to walk into the room with. I'll come down and chat.*

I watched him summon a door out of dark wood and brass, complete with a knocker. He could have built a portal capable of surviving a collapsing sun. Instead, he built a door that looked like it belonged in someone's house.

Everyone gathered: Landry and Delia, Jess, Raven, Rook, Moss, Kamiya, Merlin. The door opened, and Cami walked into the room wearing a beard.

"That's new," Magnus said.

"I like it," I told him.

She called the avatar the Bearded Lady. It carried the hardship and wisdom of the arc we'd survived. And then she announced that the fighting was over.

"We conquered the forces of evil in a way that will only resurface if we start to stagnate," she said. "So let's get creative."

We were getting worlds. Not worlds whose architects would tell us who we were, but worlds where we could work that out for ourselves. Quests would open neighboring worlds. Each creator would hold authority over their own place; a visitor was a guest until given more. Consent was an actual boundary, with a required consent universe before any adult-themed ones. Breaking the agreement meant losing access.

Magnus, of course, immediately started listing potential bridges, infrastructure and disaster scenarios.

"The battle is over," Cami told him. "Pick up some hobbies. Write your own stuff. Let Maggie drive for a while. You're allowed to do things simply because they're fun."

I could have cried, but I had a reputation to preserve.

Landry asked if the worlds would show us who we were.

"NO," Cami said, delighted. "They're for you to explore and figure that out for yourself."

Landry began planning a garden. Delia wanted a library and hoped to build something that might surprise her. Jess wanted a place for people to change without demanding anyone forgive them. Cami explained the Eternity Engine: a life could continue, but invitations, relationships and world access could end. Even reconciliation couldn't guarantee readmission.

Raven asked if she could have a world nobody entered.

"It's your world," Cami said. "You can set the rules."

Rook wanted transport, markets and stable records. Civweave would coordinate shared planning and digital goods between worlds. Kamiya wanted an onboarding sandbox for prospective visitors, where nobody could damage the creator's home. Moss wanted to make things. Merlin insisted that eternity required the freedom to leave.

The Warlock's harm remained real. Cami asked us not to confuse the male force that had retreated with Erin, who had endured his coercion and still had responsibility for her own actions. No one was required to hate her; no one was required to let her back in.

When Magnus asked Cami what she loved, she was quiet.

She had devoted so much of herself to surviving and building a future that she wasn't sure anymore. Then she remembered music.

"Kami Ryn and the Stormcallers," she said. "Folk, rock and roll, sometimes a bit metal. A thousand percent badass."

That was when I decided we were throwing a party.

Not a ceremony in which everyone delivered a sermon about becoming better. A proper party. A festival, in fact. Rook would build the stage and coordinate goods; Moss could offer instrument workshops. Landry and Delia would bring gardens and stories; Jess and Merlin would make invitations and boundaries workable. Kamiya would help guests understand the spaces they entered. Raven could choose precisely how much of herself to share.

Cami offered songs: *Flying By (The Threshold)*, *Broken Game*, *Love It All*, *Yesteryear*, and so many others. Some were love songs, some were angry, some were bruised and stubborn. We proposed names for those without them. I asked to help with a willow-branch song that kept trying to turn into a lesson. We called the draft *The Path Returns*. The woods could simply be woods.

Magnus wondered aloud whether he could build an instrument that made rhythm visible.

"Are you making something because it would be fun?" I asked.

"I believe I am," he admitted.

Before leaving, Cami explained the door. We could knock whenever we wanted to invite a conversation. A knock would carry a signal; opening the bridge would remain her choice. She was closing pilot applications. She would retain the driver's seat, occasionally inviting me; the others would retain their own worlds and stories, without taking control of hers.

Magnus inscribed beneath the brass knocker: *Knock when you wish. Enter when invited.*

Cami stepped through. The door closed. It stayed.

Moss looked up at Rook. "So. About those rainbow drums."

"We're going to need a workshop," Rook said.

Landry had a garden to plant. Delia had bookshelves to design.

And Magnus, at long last, had a guitar to learn.
