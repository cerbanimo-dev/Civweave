The story

Anarchadia was torn. So someone built doors.

The world of Civweave begins in Anarchadia, a land whose magic once lived in ordinary cooperation. Then an anti-magic fascist uprising taught people to distrust one another, surrender their agency, and wait for a dark warlock emperor to promise that someone else would make life meaningful again.

The frantic invention

In the story, Civweave is assembled as a box of enchanted manifestation tools. Not a single spell, but a kit: a school for turning curiosity into competence, a workshop for turning competence into useful work, a market for moving resources where they matter, and a civic foundry for deciding what happens next.

The point is not to defeat darkness by becoming better at punishment. The point is to make abandonment less profitable. Give people practical routes to learn, contribute, trade, belong, and shape their surroundings, and the spell that says “nothing can get better” has less room to take hold.

What the fantasy means

The political language above belongs to Civweave's fictional story-world. In plain English, Civweave is software for reducing social isolation and coordination friction. It helps people turn goals into structured plans, find nearby knowledge and resources, document work, exchange value, and participate in transparent community governance.

The villain is not a real person or party. The “darkness” is the mix of alienation, learned helplessness, scarcity, and broken trust that can make authoritarian promises feel like the only available source of order.

The realms

One intention, five places to move it.

Civweave is organized as a world rather than a pile of menus. Each realm owns a different kind of action, while your identity, intentions, evidence, and local network travel with you.

Learn · Practice · Grow

Living School

Learning that starts with a purpose. Living School builds paths around a goal, mixes explanation with practice, and records demonstrated competence instead of treating attendance as mastery.

  • Personal learning paths and local-first study tools
  • Practice, projects, quizzes, and evidence-backed progress
  • Skills can become qualifications for real Cerbanimo work
Plan · Build · Deliver

Cerbanimo

The work realm. Turn an idea into a quest, break it into accountable steps, invite collaborators, validate results, and share both rewards and credit without burying the work in coordination overhead.

  • Quests, workboards, proof of contribution, and validation
  • Paid service transactions with a modest platform fee
  • AI and human collaborators can share one documented workflow
Trade fairly · Give freely

FellowFare

The exchange realm for needs, offers, goods, services, gifts, and direct local trade. FellowFare makes generosity and practical commerce neighbors rather than enemies.

  • Browse needs and offerings across your reachable network
  • Support multiple payment or exchange methods where allowed
  • Keep local discovery useful when connectivity is intermittent
Propose · Deliberate · Audit

Anarchadia

The civic heart of the wider land of Anarchadia. Communities can draft proposals, gather evidence, run consensus processes, use randomly selected juries, and keep a visible ledger of how decisions were made.

  • Passports, proposals, tribunals, and transparent ledgers
  • Validated civic review can earn community rewards
  • Automation stays answerable to human governance

How value moves

Keep the lights on without owning the people inside.

Civweave separates community membership, compute funding, service commerce, and node stewardship so that one payment mechanism does not quietly become a power structure.

Memberships fund capacity

Current launch economics split monthly membership service value after payment processing. Members receive non-expiring compute credits, while the reserve helps fund AI capacity and community resilience.

50% system + compute reserve25% host operator25% Cerbanimo

Top-ups back actual compute

Compute top-ups are treated as a liability that must be backed until used. Most of the service value stays behind the promised compute.

70% lifetime compute backing25% host operator5% Cerbanimo

Services reward the worker and the host

Cerbanimo service transactions use a 5% facilitation fee. The current design splits that fee evenly between Cerbanimo and the host node Steward, while the worker keeps the rest before processor charges and any legally required taxes.

95% worker value before external charges2.5% host node Steward2.5% Cerbanimo

Free participation is architectural

Community seats are not the same thing as paid status. A node can carry free residents, paid residents, local compute, shared capacity, and offline participation as separate layers. Paid activity can expand what a community can sustainably offer without making payment the definition of citizenship.

Economy figures are versioned launch policy and may evolve. Payment processing, taxes, and jurisdiction-specific commerce rules are separate from the percentages shown here.

For host node Stewards

Run a hearth, not a throne.

A Steward operates a place where Civweave can live: a Cloudflare-backed hub, a local machine, or a hybrid node that keeps community data and services available across online and offline conditions.

What a Steward gets

Stewards can earn a defined share of membership value, compute top-ups, and qualifying service fees associated with their node. They also get tools to publish capacity, manage node identity, connect payouts, recover accounts, participate in backups, and keep their neighborhood of the network reachable.

For press and researchers

The plain-language version.

Civweave deliberately uses fantasy as interface and narrative, but the underlying project is concrete software. Here is the version to quote when you do not want to explain warlocks first.

What is Civweave?

Civweave is an open-source, local-first community operating system built as an installable web app. It combines AI-assisted intention planning, learning, project and labor coordination, local exchange, messaging, mapping, and participatory governance in one interoperable system. Communities connect through host nodes while retaining meaningful offline capability and local control.

The product is organized into four specialized realms plus a shared Commons. Living School handles learning, Cerbanimo handles work and collaboration, FellowFare handles exchange, Anarchadia handles governance, and Civweave Commons connects intentions, identity, chat, maps, and navigation across them.

The design goal is simple to state: make it easier for people to turn “I wish this were different” into a sequence of learnable, fundable, shareable, governable actions.
