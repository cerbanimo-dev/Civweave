# Guild democratic governance — v1

## Authority and electorate

Every active Guild resident may read the Guild Council. Opening a proposal requires a **verified contribution in the preceding 30 days**. Current qualifying evidence is either an *awarded physical-node check-in* or a *real published Guild Quest followed by independently approved completed-work review*. Merely publishing a Quest is not evidence of completed work and no longer grants proposal rights. The Guild must not count login activity, AI inference, neuron spending, a client-supplied claim, or generated text as a verified contribution. Completed-work claims require a canonical published Guild Quest ID, authenticated resident, a bounded evidence statement, and distinct resident reviewers (one independent reviewer for a two-member Guild, two for three or more; no single member may self-award). Both accepted and rejected work require the applicable review quorum. Rejected claims can be corrected and resubmitted. The case authority uses append-only accepted-work receipts; reviews represent peer attestation, not independent verification of real-world truth.

At proposal creation the server snapshots **all currently enrolled members active within the preceding 30 days**, including members who logged in but have no verified contribution. Recent verified contributions qualify a resident to **propose**, not to unilaterally define the voting electorate. Every active member has one vote, including the proposed target. Case statements and the member's response are visible inside the authenticated Guild Council, not included in a cross-Guild relay. A strict majority of this active membership snapshot (`floor(N/2)+1`) must approve each decision. For member removal, bans, and nearby-Guild reports, verified contributors first open a case with a 20–1,000-character allegation. The target may respond in Guild Council before the 24-hour notice period closes. No punitive ballot can start until at least 24 hours after case opening; afterward any qualifying contributor can sponsor the matching case as a ballot, and that ballot's sponsor casts their own yes vote. A vote executes automatically on majority as before. The notice is visible on the Guild Council service, with member-scoped unread status and read acknowledgments, but no push-notification or proof-of-delivery guarantee exists in this version. Ballots remain open seven days or until approval/rejection; no owner key, Guildkeeper role or local UI can set votes on someone else's behalf. A lone contributing Guild member cannot use removal, ban or report against themselves as a shortcut; they may voluntarily leave through the normal session/seat release endpoint. The majority requirement applies to **delegated Guildkeeper role changes** as well as removal, ban, report and any early reversal of a ban. Repeat approved reports about the same resident are limited to one in 30 days to reduce targeted harassment.

## Member notice inbox and acknowledgment

The existing canonical Guild Capacity Durable Object now derives personal notifications from server-owned disciplinary cases addressed to the current member, ballots whose frozen electorate includes the member, and pending independent-work reviews authored by someone else. The authenticated Guild Council presents these notices and an unread count. `ack-notice` creates an idempotent SQLite acknowledgment bound to the authenticated resident; another member cannot acknowledge a target's case, and acknowledgments never cast a vote, waive rights, or change the existing 24-hour notice minimum. This is a pull-based inbox, **not push delivery, confirmed reading, or proof that a member saw a notice**. Voting does not depend on clicking acknowledge, and the absence of an acknowledgment is visible rather than guessed.

## Banned resident's formal appeal

A member whose device session was revoked by a community-approved ban can file **one appeal per active ban** through `POST /api/guild/governance/appeal`. This route does not accept a bearer login as a substitute for the original resident identity: the Guild Worker independently hashes the submitted original device credential and matches the stored ban's original user ID, credential hash and approved ban ballot. The original credential stays on the client and is never returned by the API. Appeal text is bounded to 20–2,000 characters. The appeal remains a **request for reconsideration**, not an automatic pardon or a new voting identity.

All authenticated Guild members may read the appeal inside Council. A recently verified contributor can sponsor one majority-approved `lift-ban` ballot linked to it. A successful vote lifts the ban immediately through the existing durable transactional owner; rejected votes leave the original ban in force. Appellants are not able to vote while banned, and neither the form nor the report relay transmits the appeal to neighboring Guilds. The original device credentials are required; if lost, this version cannot cryptographically prove the claimant's identity. Separate appeals for removal without ban and post-delivery report corrections remain future work.

## Execution upon approval

The canonical Cloudflare Guild Capacity Durable Object exclusively owns members, proof receipts, delegated roles, sanctions and ballots. The deciding vote and resulting action are committed in one SQLite Durable Object transaction. A majority-approved vote immediately:

- **Role transfer**: grants or revokes the delegated `guildkeeper` role for a current resident. The founding credential remains separately owned and is never copied, transferred or leaked. Delegated Guildkeepers may use existing physical-node and Guildkeeper management features but **cannot** remove/ban/report people or change roles without a new member vote.
- **Remove**: invalidates the target's active Guild sessions and releases their Guild seat. Does not impose a future admission ban.
- **Ban**: performs removal and applies a 90-day block to the current resident ID and existing device credential hash. A separate, majority-approved **Lift ban** ballot can reverse it early, including when the banned person is no longer a resident. This is not a verified universal identity; a determined person can try to use a wholly new device/identity, and the system must not promise otherwise.
- **Report**: writes a privacy-limited approved report receipt to the automatic dispatch outbox. Configured public-directory transport verifies the source Guild's receipt against the registered public edge, then fans out a bounded notice to **registered Guilds within 25 km** of the reporting Guild's attested public pin. Only the reason category, reporting Guild, time, opaque pseudonym and approved-receipt reference are exposed; no name, raw resident ID, personal evidence, coordinate or private conversation is transmitted. A report is explicitly an **unverified allegation**, not proof, and never automatically bans someone at a receiving Guild.

The old founding-owner `remove-member` management action now fails with HTTP 403 and the founding UI routes to Council. Server checks cannot be bypassed by modifying the browser. Voluntary self-departure still works.

## Relay and rollout

The Guild Cloud needs `CIVWEAVE_GUILD_REPORT_RELAY_URL` set to the corresponding environment's HTTPS core directory endpoint ending `/api/guild-directory/report`, plus the same confidential 40–200-character `CIVWEAVE_GUILD_REPORT_RELAY_TOKEN` secret on the Guild Cloud Worker and the core directory Worker. Both endpoint requests and inbox retrievals require the server-only Bearer token; ordinary browsers and unauthenticated API clients are rejected. Neither setting belongs in public client JavaScript. Both are intentionally **unset by default**. If unconfigured, a successful report ballot creates the approved outbox receipt, but no actual inter-Guild delivery is falsely claimed. Once configured, the same request attempts transmission automatically; subsequent authenticated Council reads retry unresolved items. Delivery is recorded separately from approval. A Guild with no registered neighbours has zero recipients rather than inventing delivered notices. Require core migration `0016_guild_peer_reports.sql`, registration in the existing directory, independently verified source Guild status/receipt and valid public map pins on both sides.

The core directory stores a per-recipient, expiring report for 30 days; receiving Guild members can view warnings in Council without automatic sanctions. The directory's authenticated server-to-server report inbox contains only pseudonymous aggregate notices, not the member's raw identity. Guild Cloud verifies the viewing resident session and proxies the directory response rather than revealing the relay token to the browser. This initial protocol does **not** establish a portable cross-Guild user identity or an independent evidence adjudication / appeal system. Those limitations should be addressed before treating cross-Guild reporting as operational abuse prevention. Because a real report affects people's reputation, staging must be reviewed with a test directory and test Guilds before configuring production relay. Respect the existing requirement for human approval before high-stakes deployment.

## Notice and contribution limitations

- The 24-hour waiting period prevents immediate sanctions, but is **not** proof that a target has seen the case. Delivery/read acknowledgements and offline notifications are future work; Guilds should not claim guaranteed due-process notice until those exist.
- Independent work reviews prevent self-awarding but do not defeat sybil identities, collusion, or forged real-world evidence. Reviewers attest completion; no automatic model judgment establishes truth.
- A case response does not itself constitute an appeal after an executed sanction. Banned residents can now file a credential-verified formal appeal and ask a contributor to sponsor a majority-approved `lift-ban` ballot. Restoring removed membership, reversing delivered reports/corrections, independent appeals with evidence review, and recovery for lost device credentials still need further design and testing before production rollout.
- Real-world sanctions and peer reports remain operator-gated in production, and Cloudflare staging deployment/physical node parity require separate checks.

## Limited same-device anti-sybil protection

A new Guild capacity login refuses creating another enrolled account with the *same canonical SHA-256 device credential hash* and a different resident ID. The active electorate also de-duplicates legacy resident rows that share a credential, deterministically preferring the earliest account. An account still retains its own authenticated token and user ID; this is a defense against accidental duplicate votes and reused credentials, **not personhood verification**. An actor can create a new independent device credential, so no proof of one human/one vote is implied. Do not make automated high-stakes claims of identity uniqueness without a separate privacy-reviewed identity protocol.

## Appeals after ordinary removal

Community-approved removal now keeps a limited Guild-only record of the removed resident's original device credential hash, node ID and decision reference. A removed resident may submit one credential-authenticated appeal against that removal within 90 days, even without an active Guild session; mismatched credentials are rejected. No private credential is returned to the Council.

A recent verified contributor may sponsor the appeal into a frozen-electorate `restore-member` majority vote. The deciding vote automatically approves restoration and re-enrolls the original Guild identity **when a community seat is available**, without fabricating a login session, bypassing capacity, or restoring old elevated roles. The device must explicitly sign in again. If all seats are taken when approval occurs, the vote marks a restoration approved but waiting for a seat; an ordinary future login can use available capacity. Thus approval is not represented as completed restoration when the Guild is full. A removed resident using an entirely new device/identity remains outside the scope of cryptographic proof.

## In-app notice outside Guild Council

Unread Council notice count is projected through a single existing Guild access surface. The canonical Council module queries only on selected-Guild/session/visibility transitions and after a Council interaction, then emits a narrow `civweave:guild-council-notices` event. The Guild access view shows the count beside its existing Council button. This is an **event-driven in-app badge, not Web Push, email or OS push**, and does not guarantee a notice has reached the device.

## Isolated staging Worker deployment

The `deploy-staging-mobile-guild-governance.yml` workflow deploys the canonical Guild Capacity implementation to distinct Cloudflare Worker `civweave-guild-governance-staging`, with isolated Durable Object namespace and `CIVWEAVE_ENVIRONMENT=staging`. Its config never points at `civweave-guild-cloud` or the production Guild Worker. The workflow runs with the existing staging Cloudflare account credentials (the same account used by staging Pages/core), fails if an inter-Guild report relay secret is already configured, and checks unauthenticated access is rejected. Its separate Worker origin is `https://civweave-guild-governance-staging.cerbanimo.workers.dev`. It is intended for staging-test Guilds only; it does not rewrite existing production Guilds' server selection. Cloudflare Pages staging is separately deployed by `deploy-civweave-staging.yml` on a staging merge. Check both workflows and the staging artifact marker before claiming live deployment.
