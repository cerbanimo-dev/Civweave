# Territory Steward Inauguration Ritual v1

A short public rite for welcoming a Territory Steward and recording the Steward's election of a Second.

The ceremony may use the participants' chosen public names. Contract, tax, payout, and identity records remain separate administrative records and use whatever verified identity information those systems require.

## Roles

- **Convener** — conducts the rite and keeps the record.
- **Incoming Territory Steward** — accepts stewardship of the named territory.
- **Second** — freely accepts election as the Steward's trusted deputy, witness, and continuity partner.
- **Witnesses** — members present in person or remotely.

The Second is not automatically entitled to the Steward's territorial share merely by holding the role. The territorial allocation belongs to the active stewardship office. If stewardship changes, the allocation follows the recognized successor to that office under the applicable governance process.

## What to bring

Use any three simple objects that are available:

1. a length of thread, cord, yarn, or ribbon for the territory;
2. a small object representing local work, such as a tool, seed, coin, stone, or piece of craft;
3. the stewardship record, digital or physical.

No special object is required for validity. The objects are mnemonic, not magical or legal prerequisites.

## Opening: Name the territory

The Convener states:

> We gather to place care before possession, responsibility before title, and continuity before personality. We name this territory: **[TERRITORY]**.

The Convener places the thread between the Incoming Steward and the witnesses.

> This thread is not a border. It is a promise to notice what happens here, to keep the weave connected, and to leave the territory capable of continuing without any one person.

## The Steward's undertaking

The Convener asks:

> **[STEWARD NAME]**, do you accept stewardship of **[TERRITORY]** for as long as you are willing and able to serve it?

The Incoming Steward answers:

> I accept.

The Convener asks:

> Will you tend the territory without claiming ownership of its people; protect the freedom to participate without payment; keep its economic rules legible; preserve the Host Node Stewards' promised shares; and use the Territory Stewardship share for the health, reach, and continuity of the territory?

The Incoming Steward answers:

> I will.

The Convener asks:

> When you can no longer serve, will you return the office cleanly, so its responsibilities and territorial share can pass to the next recognized Steward rather than becoming your permanent property?

The Incoming Steward answers:

> I will return what was entrusted to me.

The Incoming Steward takes or touches the local-work object.

## The election of a Second

The Convener says:

> No Steward should be a single point of failure. Choose now the person you trust to question you, cover for you, remember what you forget, and help the territory survive your absence.

The Incoming Steward says:

> I elect **[SECOND NAME]** as my Second for **[TERRITORY]**.

The Convener asks the proposed Second:

> **[SECOND NAME]**, do you freely accept this election?

The Second answers:

> I accept.

The Convener asks:

> Will you advise without flattering, disagree without sabotaging, preserve the record, step in when needed, and help ensure that stewardship can be transferred without fracture?

The Second answers:

> I will.

The Steward and Second each hold one end of the thread.

The Steward says:

> You are not beneath me. You stand beside the work.

The Second says:

> I will help keep the work larger than either of us.

## Witness and record

The Convener asks the witnesses:

> Do you recognize **[STEWARD NAME]** as Territory Steward of **[TERRITORY]**, and **[SECOND NAME]** as their elected Second, subject to the continuing rules and governance of the network?

Witnesses answer:

> We recognize them.

The Convener records:

- Territory
- Steward public name
- Steward internal identity/reference, if applicable
- Second public name
- Second internal identity/reference, if applicable
- Effective date and time
- Witnesses or quorum reference
- Stewardship agreement/version
- Economic-model version
- Any term, review date, or local conditions

The Steward and Second may tie one loose knot in the thread together. It should be a knot that can be untied.

The Convener says:

> The knot is deliberate and reversible. So is stewardship.

## Inauguration

The Convener gives the local-work object to the Steward and says:

> **[STEWARD NAME]**, you are inaugurated as Territory Steward of **[TERRITORY]**. Tend the local weave, keep its doors visible, keep its accounts understandable, and make yourself replaceable.

Then to the Second:

> **[SECOND NAME]**, you are recognized as Second. Keep another copy of the map.

Then to both:

> The territory is not yours. The responsibility is.

The ceremony closes with:

> **May the work circulate. May the credit follow the work. May the capacity return to the people who keep it alive.**

## Administrative closeout

Immediately after the ceremony:

1. record the Steward and Second in the stewardship registry;
2. attach the applicable stewardship agreement;
3. record the territory identifier and effective timestamp;
4. verify any payout/KYC information required by the payment provider before disbursement;
5. activate the Territory Stewardship allocation only for transactions settling on or after the effective timestamp;
6. publish the public stewardship record without exposing private identity, tax, banking, or payout credentials;
7. give both Steward and Second access to the continuity materials needed to transfer the office cleanly.

## Succession

If the Steward resigns, is removed under the applicable governance process, dies, becomes unreachable, or otherwise ceases to hold the office, the Territory Stewardship allocation stops belonging to that Steward at the effective end of their tenure. It follows the active stewardship office once a successor is recognized.

The Second is the default continuity contact and may be nominated or considered first for succession, but the title of Second alone does not silently convert into permanent Stewardship unless the governing rules explicitly make that transition.
