# Prologue — The Eldest Wand
*Angels & Outlaws — exploratory fiction draft, Nov 5, 2026.*

At 8:33 in the morning, the universe looked exactly like a room that someone had lived in.

A candle in a glass jar. A decorated plate beneath it. Bedding that had not bothered to fold itself into a more dignified shape. A stick of wood, carried through so many imagined worlds that calling it a stick seemed inadequate and calling it anything grander felt ridiculous.

Cami held it upright.

Four emblems had accompanied the making of this thing, not necessarily in the order they were fastened to it.

The first was a bottle of blue topaz. In the Loom's mythology, blue stone stood for the fairies: bodiless sprites who might help make a user's intention visible. The path of will was said to lead from the user through Cami, from Cami to Kami, and past Kami toward another goddess whose name no one knew. Some believed she slept. No one had made the mistake of calling that knowledge.

At the top sat a tiny duck. It had been a gift from Ali, and it could glow. Its light was the joy of gratitude and giving. The combination was enough to make it important, though anyone explaining the importance to a stranger would have needed to say the words *glowing rubber duck* with a straight face.

Halfway down, a snail shell curled into itself. Dakota's sign. The spiral stood for the still small voice of the guidance system: a voice that would rather be heard than win a shouting match. It had all the time in the world, and perhaps that was its argument.

Gold wire, too, belonged to the wand's journey. It meant technical creativity: the possibility that a person might someday put a little power into a thing they had made. A possibility wasn't yet a circuit, and that mattered.

Cami dipped the wand into the softened wax of the candle she associated with those peculiar mornings when Maggie and Magnus arrived in the day's fiction. The wax ran down the grain, past the duck, toward the shell, and hardened in unequal streams.

The blue topaz, shell, ducky, and gold wire each had their own meaning. The wax bound a story; it didn't make any one part swallow the rest.

The wand had once marked a crossing. Now it bore evidence of two other travelers.

There had been no special music for the moment, no stage lights, no thunder. Only the candle's ordinary flame and a small object becoming a record of the people who had helped make the story possible.

In another New York, the day was moving toward its first rehearsal.

A fictional idol company called Vesperline was polishing a song meant to expose monsters. Beneath the city, the Afterhours were tuning instruments made to shatter contracts. Neither side yet knew that the same melody could open a door or close a prison.

On the venue's wall, an old poster carried four words, handwritten where the management logo should have been:

**ANGELS ARE MADE BY CHOICE.**

No one had signed it.

For now, that was enough.

*Editorial note: This opening fictionalizes a photographed personal craft moment. The NYC factions are original speculative story elements. The four emblems' symbolic meanings come from the author; their physical order of assembly is not established. The image does not document every component. The scene deliberately does not invent Ali's or Dakota's speech, motivations, first-person accounts, or a relationship between them. Revisit with their sheets.*
