# Angels & Outlaws
*A new standalone / connected novel in The Blade & Mirror Saga's post–Book VI Maggieverse.*

**Status:** foundation / early concept, awaiting the author's separate character sheets and first-person accounts for Ali and Dakota.  
**Created:** 2026-11-05.  
**Author:** Cami Ryn Tharp.  
**Genre:** adult dark romance; supernatural urban fantasy; K-pop performance thriller; demon redemption; anti-vampire pop-punk noir.  
**Primary stage:** an alternate, fictional New York City; expandable into separate Ali and Dakota universes.  
**Continuity:** post–Book VI; no retcon of earlier books.

## Logline
In the fictional city where hit songs are weapons and the biggest K-pop idols hunt demons, a Turn can transform a demon into an angel—but no one agrees who deserves a second chance. Two scouts, **Ali the Angel** and **Dakota the Daemon**, follow an anomalous signal into New York's underground music scene. A homemade wand crowned by a glowing rubber ducky and a spiral snail shell may connect the worlds that the city's hunters have kept apart.

The heart of this book is dangerous love, self-determination, transformation, and the choice to become better without being owned.

## Book promise
A song can expose the shape of a soul. A concert can become a rescue. Love raises the stakes, but romance never eliminates a character's right to refuse. No demon is obligated to Turn, and no angel gains authority over another person just by being called an angel.

## Story engine — provisional, subject to the character sheets
**The Vesperline**: a fictional K-pop collective trained to detect demonic interference in performances. Their highest art is not killing; it is creating conditions where a demon *can choose* the Turn.

**The Afterhours**: anti-vampires from a punk/gothic night circuit. Instead of drinking blood, these fictional anti-vampires interrupt predatory bargains and metabolize the hunger that powers them. Aesthetic reference: theatrical, witty, aching pop-punk energy. These are original fictional characters, not Fall Out Boy members.

**The Velvet Contract**: a corrupt performance syndicate that profits from keeping demons trapped as antagonists. It sells idol-hunter contracts and black-market salvation packages while burying the fact that choice is essential to a real Turn.

**The Eldest Wand**: not a crown, a weapon of compulsory salvation, or a universal mind-control device; it connects possible paths through the Loom. The duck and shell motifs serve as recognizably separate signals associated with Ali and Dakota. It is not assumed to create real-world supernatural effects.

## Twelve-chapter provisional architecture
1. **8:33 — The Eldest Wand**: documented making of the symbolic artifact; a signal reaches fictional NYC.
2. **Angels on the Night Train**: the two scouts locate a music scene where stage and underworld overlap.
3. **The Idol Who Missed a Note**: a performance reveals a demon the hunters are ordered to destroy.
4. **The Afterhours Set**: anti-vampire outlaws interrupt the contract and offer an alternative.
5. **Velvet Teeth**: the first evidence of coerced 'redemption' emerges.
6. **The Duck and the Spiral**: Ali and Dakota's linked-but-distinct paths lead to the same hidden door.
7. **A Love Song with Conditions**: the romance storyline confronts secrets, attraction, and boundaries.
8. **The Angel Audit**: the characters distinguish consent, accountability, public reputation, and actual change.
9. **Encore in the Underworld**: the hunt becomes a rescue with real costs.
10. **The Outlaw Chorus**: rival performers and demons choose whether to cooperate.
11. **The Turn**: an adult character who has faced their harm freely chooses transformation.
12. **Angels & Outlaws**: the Velvet Contract is exposed; the new world remains messy, alive, and open.
