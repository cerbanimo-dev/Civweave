# Five-act outline — The Girls Who Stole Tomorrow
*Author-review proposal, not a changed canonical Blade & Mirror timeline.*

## Core engine
A mysterious calendar failure is the visible symptom of a cosmic market: wealthy buyers purchase guarantees of desired futures, while alternate outcomes and their potential residents are being sequestered in a Library Beyond Tomorrow. The apparent hero of that scheme is a Landry who has prevented so much suffering that she now treats every unscripted choice as negligence. Our Landry fights for a future that includes genuine uncertainty.

The world and all characters remain distinct and autonomous. A hopeful ending cannot depend on abolishing consent or erasing harm already experienced.

## Act I — The Day Tomorrow Disappeared
**Opening:** Landry's appointment, a printed cat calendar, and the town's inability to schedule a day beyond midnight. Delia questions what is observable. Magnus and Maggie join by a voluntary call handoff. At a diner, a seven-year-old presents six sugar packets for six claimed rescues and a seventh yet to come.

**First threshold:** The photograph shows Landry, Delia, Magnus and Maggie physically separate at an unfamiliar building, with a fifth face cut away. The diner screens announce midnight bidding. Landry refuses to make promises to the child before learning their cost.

**Questions:** Is a date actually missing? Is the child from the future, or repeating instructions? Why is Delia separately pictured? Establish at least two independent evidence routes before accepting supernatural explanations.

## Act II — The Highway of Unlived Lives
A door leads onto a road littered with waystations from futures never allowed to complete. There are no expendable background people: travelers have homes, friendships, and their own reasons to stay or leave. Some *like* the lives they have; some want another option. Neither response is wrong.

**Gameplay/PvE:** solve a road that returns travelers to the same exit; locate a public archive of disputed possibilities; earn permission to cross branches. World events require witnessed state and costs, not arbitrary rescue fiat.

**Character growth:** Delia challenges Landry's rescue reflex. Magnus/Merlin map the cost and rules, with Merlin as a separate consulting guide. Maggie finds the humor and corruption in an agency that has trademarked tomorrow. Gaia/Kami holds the moral line: protect permission to choose, not a particular choice.

**Midpoint preview:** An unopened tomorrow still belongs to somebody, whether or not they exist yet in the current branch.

## Act III — The Auction at the End of Time
A luxury auction offers buyers fixed happy endings. The Registrar speaks in euphemisms, and the catalog's fine print reveals that guaranteed outcomes are financed by erasing incompatible possibilities. Every sale is a theft of alternative lives.

**Maggie set piece:** enters as a sharp-tongued guest or contractor under an approved, reversible fictional disguise. Her objective is the transaction record, not an unauthorized purchase. The fruit-fly **bigfixer** identifies a maintenance opening too small for the security architecture to notice.

**Rule test:** Opening a stored life seems to collapse other potential futures. The team must identify whether this is an inherent law, a malicious implementation decision, or a lie told to support the market.

**Reversal:** Freeing the futures could expose everyone to change, grief and mistakes again. Some beneficiaries of the auction resist.

## Act IV — The Girl in the Photograph
The child's six rescues are explained with evidence and discrepancies, never as an unquestionable prophecy. The missing fifth face is finally identified only after the author approves its connection to the child's stakes.

**Spoiler reveal:** The architect of the system is a counterpart Landry who prevented every tragedy she could reach. Her desire to protect others has expanded into ownership of their lives. She may be sincere, but sincerity does not erase coercion.

**Critical decision:** Landry can seize the system and guarantee a kinder set of outcomes, or refuse to become a new owner of everyone's future. The girl insists that her future is hers to choose, even if Landry cannot predict what happens to her.

## Act V — Give Tomorrow Back
The heroes do not destroy the records and abandon their inhabitants. They open a consent-based return route, preserve identity and lived memories, log the costs, and establish a genuine appeal and exit path for anyone affected.

**Final conflict:** Not a compulsory redemption duel. The counterpart Landry must voluntarily relinquish control and face what the guarantee system did; victims owe her no forgiveness. The auction ends; people are allowed to make decisions that the heroes dislike.

**Emotional close:** Tomorrow appears again in the world's calendars with blank pages that people may fill themselves. The girl's exact destiny is left hers. Landry places the rune stone against the first blank date and chooses one ordinary plan without demanding a guarantee.

## Reveal and continuity register
- The child is a distinct possible person, not an automatic daughter or replacement self.
- The fifth figure remains an intentional unresolved mystery pending author decision.
- Landry and Delia's separate appearance in the photo needs a grounded rule and consent.
- The mechanism must distinguish a **broken future index** from physics before declaring that time itself stopped.
- The Other Landry's connection to Landry-Prime, if any, is an unapproved question; do not quietly collapse them into one person.
- The Turn crossing must preserve identity, distinctness, memory continuity and refusal rights.
- Avoid villain exoneration through cosmic mechanics, involuntary merges, and forced memory erasure.
- Preserve saga series title, POV rules, reserve the single F-word for the final Magnus line of Part I, and avoid mechanical oppositional phrasing.
