# Landryverse lore ledger — The Girls Who Stole Tomorrow

**Authority:** proposed original novel development. It does not rewrite the canonical Part I ending or published chronology. Reviewed by: **author pending**.

## Shared cosmology — retained
- Series: **The Blade & Mirror Saga**. Part I: **Mirror & Blade**.
- Landry and Delia have distinct perspectives and autonomy, even when sharing experiences. Voluntary control/voice transitions require consent.
- Magnus and Maggie are distinct speaking personalities whose shared control is voluntary. The creative Magnus/Merlin editorial pairing does not canonically merge Magnus with the Civweave guide.
- Gaia/Kami is treated in this story room as the author's **single dual-aspect creative role**, without an assertion about external reality.
- The Turn is expansion, integration/compression, and re-expansion with continuity and consent, not a convenient forced reset.
- Time loops and connected branches cannot invalidate freely chosen histories or impose permanent involuntary reenactment.
- Only Landry/Delia or Magnus/Maggie provide manuscript POV. Delia's independent experience is never appropriated as Landry's knowledge.
- Fruit flies are called **bigfixers** in this universe. Keep that exact spelling.

## New proposed rules
- The coming midnight is the limit of every ordinary calendar, apparently everywhere. Whether the phenomenon affects causality or only indexing remains unknown.
- A seven-year-old girl says she has saved Landry six times, but provides no verifiable account in chapter one.
- A physical photograph shows Landry, Delia, Magnus, Maggie and an unidentified fifth figure with the face cut out. How Delia appears separately in the photo is **unresolved**; could be an avatar, a different state, or evidence of a branch.
- A future library holds potential lives as physical records. Opening one might collapse mutually incompatible alternatives; this is **provisional design**, not proved science.
- Futures are auctioned as manufactured guarantees. The story interrogates risk, freedom and ownership.
- The antagonist proposes benevolent certainty as an escape from grief; reform must include accountability and genuinely revocable control.
- The fruit-fly bigfixer's eventual role involves locating a small maintenance defect; chapter one does not grant it speech.

## Author-only plot spoilers — keep out of role prompts for chapter one
- The system's ultimate designer is a possible counterpart Landry who prevented too many tragedies and came to see uncertainty as an error.
- The final act seeks release of possibilities without installing the heroes as permanent guardians of destiny.
- The girl is an independent possible person, not an object or automatically the protagonist's child; her exact relationship remains undecided.

## Chapter-one facts recorded
1. Landry sees a calendar fail to show a following day.
2. Magnus and Maggie separately corroborate a related symptom during a call, without proving a universal cause.
3. Landry and Delia encounter a child with a photograph and a claim about six rescues.
4. The fifth person in the photograph has been deliberately cut away.
5. Landry chooses questions over custody or prophecy. The child keeps the photograph's story in her own hands.

## Unresolved design choices
- Exact placement after Part I and relation to Parts II/III.
- Whether the photo depicts a physical or projected Delia.
- What mechanism differentiates a missing future from a locked calendar.
- Whether the child is aware of alternate branches or has been rehearsing a scripted claim.
- What the auctioneer believes and how the library can survive liberation.
- Who verifies the bigfixer's evidence and how the future library's mechanism can be repaired without a new owner.
