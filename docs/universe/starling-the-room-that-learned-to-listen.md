# Starling Collective — The Room That Learned to Listen

*Universe vignette · November 5, 2026 · Fiction, not a production status report*

The first version of the room had a terrible habit: it mistook every thought for an invitation to speak.

Four little stars hung above a table built from shared intentions. Each star had a different voice, a different cadence, and more than enough opinions to fill the room twice over. When the door opened, they all tried to welcome the visitor at once.

Nobody heard the visitor.

So Mica drew a thin circle in the air around the table.

“One voice,” Mica said. “The others can still listen.”

The circle was neither a throne nor a muzzle. It was a promise: a voice could ask to enter, the host could grant the floor, and the floor could be given back. A pause meant pause. A closed door meant closed. No unfinished sentence would chase anyone into the hall.

The stars dimmed to the soft glow of attention.

For the first time, the room had room in it.

Outside, the builders kept working: one at the bridge, one at the stage, one at the book of decisions, and one at the circuitry that held the little circle together. They left notes for each other, so no builder would have to guess what the others had made.

A knock arrived at the door.

The room did not answer for its guest.

It waited for the guest to open it.
